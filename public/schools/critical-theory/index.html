Critical theory (Frankfurt School)

Social theory should not just describe society but expose the forms of domination it hides, with a view to human emancipation.

The question

Why did reason and progress produce new forms of domination?

Why it arose

The Institute for Social Research was founded in Frankfurt in 1923, and its thinkers soon asked why the socialist revolution Marx predicted had not come in the West. Mostly Jewish, they watched workers turn to fascism and fled Nazi Germany; in American exile they confronted mass culture and advertising.

The reasoning

Horkheimer distinguished “traditional” theory, which treats society as a given object, from “critical” theory, which asks whose interests the existing order serves. Horkheimer and Adorno argued that Enlightenment reason, reduced to a tool for controlling nature and people, can turn into its opposite. Habermas later found a counterweight in communication: norms are legitimate only if all affected could agree to them in free discussion.

Key claims
  1. Theory should serve emancipation, not just description.
  2. Instrumental reason can turn enlightenment into new domination.
  3. The culture industry manufactures conformity.
What it left

It shaped the New Left, media and cultural studies, and theories of democracy built on public discussion.
