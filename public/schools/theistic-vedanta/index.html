India · 11th c. CE – today

Theistic Vedānta (Viśiṣṭādvaita & Dvaita)

Brahman is a personal God (Viṣṇu), souls and the world are real, and liberation comes through loving devotion (bhakti) and God’s grace.

The question

How can finite souls be real and still depend on one God?

Why it arose

In South India the devotional poetry of the Ālvār saints had made love of Viṣṇu central to religious life. Rāmānuja, and later Madhva, gave that devotion a philosophical defence by rereading the same Upanishads that Śaṅkara had read as strictly non-dual.

The reasoning

Rāmānuja argued that all knowledge has an object and involves distinctions, so a pure, featureless consciousness and an illusory world make no sense; souls and matter are real, as the ‘body’ of God, qualifying a single Brahman. Madhva went further: God, souls and matter are eternally different, just as scripture’s plain meaning and ordinary perception say.

Key claims
  1. The world is real, not an illusion.
  2. Souls are distinct from, and dependent on, God.
  3. Devotion (bhakti) and grace lead to liberation.
What it left

It underlies the major Vaiṣṇava traditions, including the Śrī Vaiṣṇava community, and fed later devotional movements across India.
