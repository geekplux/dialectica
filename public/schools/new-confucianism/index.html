China & East Asia · 1920s – today

New Confucianism

Confucian moral metaphysics can be renewed in dialogue with Western philosophy and can ground a Chinese modernity that includes science and democracy.

The question

Can Confucian thought survive and guide modern China?

Why it arose

The May Fourth Movement of 1919 attacked Confucianism as the root of China’s weakness, and Western science and democracy seemed to make it obsolete. Liang Shuming, Xiong Shili and Feng Youlan responded, and after 1949 Mou Zongsan and others carried the project on in Hong Kong and Taiwan.

The reasoning

Science answers questions of fact but not of value and meaning. Confucian learning of mind and nature offers a “moral metaphysics” grounded in the moral mind. So China can adopt science and democracy while keeping Confucianism as its spiritual core; Mou argued the moral mind can limit itself to make room for them.

Key claims
  1. Moral experience reveals the metaphysical reality of the mind.
  2. Cultures differ in orientation, not merely in stage of development.
  3. Confucianism can “open out” science and democracy.
What it left

It shaped Confucian studies in Hong Kong, Taiwan and the diaspora, notably through the 1958 Manifesto for Chinese Culture, and fed the revival of Confucianism in mainland China since the 1980s.
