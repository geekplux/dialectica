Continental · c. 1805 – today

Hermeneutics

Understanding is interpretation: we grasp texts, people and history by moving between parts and whole, always from within a tradition.

The question

What happens when we understand a text, a person or a past?

Why it arose

Interpretation began as a craft for reading scripture and law. Schleiermacher, amid the Romantic interest in language and individuality, turned it into a general theory of understanding; Dilthey then used it to argue that the human sciences need their own method, not the methods of physics.

The reasoning

We understand a sentence only through the whole text, and the whole only through its sentences: the “hermeneutic circle”. Gadamer added that we never stand outside history; our prejudices are not just errors but the starting point of understanding, which happens as a “fusion of horizons” between our world and the text’s.

Key claims
  1. Parts and whole illuminate each other in the hermeneutic circle.
  2. The human sciences aim at understanding, not causal explanation.
  3. All understanding is shaped by history and tradition.
What it left

It reshaped literary theory, theology, law and the social sciences, and underlies debates about whether objectivity is possible in the humanities.

On the record

Confirmed

Hermeneutics as a school is dated from 1805, Schleiermacher’s first lectures on it.

Schleiermacher first lectured on hermeneutics in 1805, while teaching at Halle, and gave the course repeatedly until 1833; his lecture notes were published after his death, in 1838.
