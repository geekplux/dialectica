The Islamic world · 9th c. – present

Sufi metaphysics

The deepest knowledge of God comes by tasting and unveiling, not by argument; for Ibn ʿArabī, God alone truly is, and creatures are God’s self-disclosures.

The question

Can God be known directly, beyond reason?

Why it arose

Sufism began as an ascetic and devotional movement seeking closeness to God. As it matured, thinkers set out the theory behind the practice: how the self is purified, and what the world must be if a mystic can find God in it.

The reasoning

Reason works by concepts that divide and limit, so it cannot grasp an infinite God; only a purified heart can receive that knowledge. And if God is the only true being, everything else exists only as a mirror in which God’s names are shown.

Key claims
  1. Direct experiential knowledge surpasses rational proof.
  2. The self must die to itself to find God.
  3. Creation is God’s self-disclosure in many forms.
What it left

It shaped Persian, Turkish and Urdu poetry and the later Islamic philosophy of Mullā Ṣadrā; the term “unity of being” (waḥdat al-wujūd) was attached to Ibn ʿArabī’s thought by later followers and critics.
