China & East Asia · c. 500 BCE – today

Confucianism

People become fully humane through learning, ritual and family relationships, and good government rests on the ruler’s virtue rather than on force.

The question

How can people live well together and be governed well?

Why it arose

In the Spring and Autumn period the Zhou order was falling apart: lords warred with one another and the old rites had become empty show. Confucius, a minor official and teacher from the state of Lu, looked back to the early Zhou as a model and tried to restore order by cultivating persons rather than by tightening laws.

The reasoning

Social order depends on people’s inner dispositions, and dispositions are formed by practice: the roles we play in the family and the rites we perform. So cultivate humaneness (ren) and ritual propriety (li) from the family outward; a ruler who is virtuous will win people over as the wind bends the grass, and punishment becomes a last resort.

Key claims
  1. Humaneness (ren) is the highest virtue.
  2. Ritual shapes character as well as expressing it.
  3. Govern by virtue and example, not by punishment.
What it left

From the Han dynasty onward it became the official learning of imperial China, and it shaped education, family life and the state across Korea, Japan and Vietnam.

On the record

Contested

Dong Zhongshu is listed as the author of the Luxuriant Dew of the Spring and Autumn Annals (“attributed”, c. 110 BCE).

The attribution has been doubted since the Song dynasty, Zhu Xi among the sceptics. The 82-chapter work is undated and is now usually treated as a collection of loosely related Han-dynasty chapters; some parts may reflect Dong’s teaching, others are later. The year is only a placeholder for “Western Han”.
