Marxism

History is driven by material production and class struggle, and capitalism, which exploits wage labour, will give way to a classless society.

The question

Why are the people who produce wealth so poor, and how can that change?

Why it arose

Industrial capitalism in 1840s Britain and Germany produced unprecedented wealth beside slums, child labour and failed revolutions. Young Hegelians such as Marx inherited Hegel’s idea that history unfolds through contradiction, but found his account too abstract to explain real misery.

The reasoning

Humans survive by working on nature, so how a society produces (its tools and property relations) shapes its laws, politics and ideas. Under capitalism workers sell their labour power for less than the value it creates, and the surplus becomes profit. Because this conflict is built into the system, Marx argued, it generates crises and eventually its own overthrow.

Key claims
  1. Material conditions shape consciousness, not the other way round.
  2. History is the history of class struggles.
  3. Capitalism alienates workers from their labour, its products and each other.
What it left

It inspired labour movements, revolutions and twentieth-century socialist states, and remains a core tool in sociology, history and the critique of capitalism.

What it led to

Carried on by
Critical theory (Frankfurt School)The Frankfurt School kept Marx’s critique of capitalism but asked why the working class had not become revolutionary.Opposed by
