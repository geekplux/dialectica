Analytic & pragmatist · 1879 – c. 1930

Early analytic philosophy

Many philosophical problems come from the misleading surface of language, and can be solved by analysing statements into their true logical form.

The question

What is the logical structure of thought, language and the world?

Why it arose

Frege’s new logic of 1879 set out to show that arithmetic rests on logic alone, giving philosophy a tool far sharper than Aristotle’s syllogisms. From 1898 in Cambridge, Russell and Moore broke with the Hegel-inspired British idealism they had been taught, and turned to logic and common sense instead.

The reasoning

Grammar can hide logical form: “The present king of France is bald” looks as if it names someone, but analysis shows it asserts that exactly one such king exists, so it is simply false. If philosophy clarifies what our sentences really say, puzzles about existence, meaning and truth can be answered precisely or dissolved. Logic thus becomes the method of philosophy.

Key claims
  1. Modern logic is the essential tool of philosophy.
  2. Grammatical form can mislead us about logical form.
  3. Analysis breaks complex propositions into simpler ones.
What it left

It founded the analytic tradition that dominates English-language philosophy, and its formal logic fed directly into the theory of computation.

On the record

Corrected

Russell and Moore broke with British idealism around 1900.

Russell dated it himself: “It was towards the end of 1898 that Moore and I rebelled against both Kant and Hegel.” Moore’s “The Nature of Judgment” (1899) and “The Refutation of Idealism” (1903) followed. The atlas now says “from 1898”, matching the Moore–Russell tie.

Sources: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Bertrand Russell” · The Cambridge Companion to Bertrand Russell, “Russell and Moore, 1898–1905”
