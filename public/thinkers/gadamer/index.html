Continental · 1900–2002

Hans-Georg Gadamer

Heidegger’s student, who made hermeneutics a philosophy of understanding

Gadamer argued that understanding is not a method but an event: we always interpret from within tradition and its prejudices, and truth emerges in a “fusion of horizons” between our present and the text.

Life and thought

Hans-Georg Gadamer (1900–2002) grew up in Breslau and Marburg, where he studied with Heidegger and in 1929 completed his habilitation under him. He spent the Nazi years teaching in Leipzig, briefly served as its rector under Soviet occupation, and settled in Heidelberg in 1949. Truth and Method appeared only in 1960, when he was sixty; he lived to 102, still lecturing and debating.

Gadamer argued that understanding is not a technique for removing our biases but something that happens to us within a tradition. We always approach a text with “prejudices”, pre-judgements inherited from history and language, and these are not only obstacles but the very conditions that let the text speak to us. Understanding is a “fusion of horizons”: our questions and the text’s world meet and both are changed, as in a good conversation. Habermas objected that tradition can also hide domination, and their debate defined post-war hermeneutics.
