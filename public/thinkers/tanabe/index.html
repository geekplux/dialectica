China & East Asia · 1885–1962

Tanabe Hajime

Kyoto School philosopher and Nishida’s critic

Criticised Nishida’s “place of nothingness” as too contemplative and proposed a “logic of species” centred on social mediation; after the war he called for “metanoetics”, philosophy as repentance.

Life and thought

Tanabe Hajime, born in Tokyo in 1885, studied mathematics and then philosophy at Tokyo Imperial University and wrote on the philosophy of science. Nishida brought him to Kyoto in 1919; in 1922–24 he studied in Germany, where he met Husserl and the young Heidegger. He succeeded Nishida in Kyoto and from 1930 openly criticised him. During the war his philosophy was used to support the state, and in 1945 he retired to a mountain village, where he wrote a book of self-criticism. He died in 1962.

His “logic of species” argued that individuals and humanity are always mediated by a “species”, a concrete community such as a nation; he meant it as social philosophy, but it lent itself to wartime nationalism. In Philosophy as Metanoetics (1946) he concluded that reason, pushed to its limits, breaks down, and that philosophy must pass through repentance (zange), relying on an “Other-power” in the spirit of Shinran’s Pure Land Buddhism, to be reborn. This turn made him a partner in later Buddhist–Christian dialogue and a model of a philosopher confronting his own complicity.
