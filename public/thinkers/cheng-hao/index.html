Dialectica / Thinkers / Cheng Hao

China & East Asia · 1032–1085

Cheng Hao

Elder Cheng brother, teacher of humaneness as oneness

The humane person forms one body with heaven, earth and the myriad things; principle is realised by quietly recognising this unity rather than by piling up book learning.

Life and thought

Cheng Hao, called Mingdao (“Clarifier of the Way”), passed the highest examination in 1057 and served as a local official, known for gentle and effective government. He opposed Wang Anshi’s sweeping New Policies, and from the 1070s he and his younger brother Cheng Yi taught in and around Luoyang. Students remembered his warmth: sitting with him, one said, was like sitting in a spring breeze. He died in 1085.

His key idea is that the humane person forms one body with heaven, earth and all things. He drew on medical language: Chinese doctors call numbness in the limbs “not humane” (buren), because the patient no longer feels his own hand as part of himself. Selfishness is that kind of numbness towards others. So the learner must first “recognise humaneness” in this lived sense and then preserve it with sincerity and reverence, without forcing it or searching outside. His intuitive emphasis on the mind’s unity with the world was later taken up by Lu Jiuyuan and Wang Yangming, while Zhu Xi built more on his brother.

On the record

Corrected

Cheng Hao’s “On Understanding Humaneness” is dated 1080.

The piece is preserved in the Surviving Works of the Two Chengs (book 2A) as Cheng Hao’s answer to Lü Dalin, who came east to study with the brothers after Zhang Zai’s death; Lü’s record of that visit is dated 1079. The year is therefore better given as c. 1079.

Sources: 曾亦、郭晓东：《程明道：本体即功夫，功夫即本体》（儒家网） · 曹树明，《哲学动态》2018年第7期（搜狐转载）

Corrected

Zhang Zai is called the Cheng brothers’ “uncle by family ties”.

Zhang Zai was the brothers’ biaoshu (表叔), an uncle through a cousin’s line, one generation above them: they were his biaozhi, and the three are remembered as kinsmen of one mind.

Sources: Wikipedia (Chinese), “張載 (北宋)”
