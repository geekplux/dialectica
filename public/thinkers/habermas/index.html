Continental · 1929–2026

Jürgen Habermas

Leading second-generation Frankfurt theorist of democracy and communication

Habermas argued that besides instrumental reason there is communicative reason, aimed at mutual understanding; legitimate norms and laws are those all affected could accept in free and equal public discussion.

Life and thought

Jürgen Habermas (1929–2026) grew up in Gummersbach under Nazism; he later said that learning the truth about the regime’s crimes after 1945 shaped his whole life’s work. He was Adorno’s assistant at the Institute for Social Research in the 1950s, taught at Heidelberg and Frankfurt, and directed a Max Planck institute in Starnberg. For six decades he intervened in German public debate, from the “historians’ dispute” over the Nazi past to European unity. He died in March 2026, aged 96.

Habermas argued that the first Frankfurt School had been too pessimistic about reason. Besides instrumental reason, which calculates how to reach goals, there is communicative reason, at work whenever people try to reach understanding by giving reasons others can accept or reject. From this he built discourse ethics: a norm is valid only if all those affected could agree to it in free and equal discussion. His study of the “public sphere” and his theory of law and democracy made him the leading defender of modernity as an unfinished project.
