Dialectica / Thinkers / Rāmānuja

India · c. 1077–1157 (traditionally 1017–1137)

Rāmānuja

Vaiṣṇava theologian and founder of Viśiṣṭādvaita

His ‘qualified non-dualism’ holds that Brahman is one but includes real souls and matter as its body; liberation is reaching God through devotion and surrender, not dissolving into featureless consciousness.

Life and thought

Rāmānuja was born in Tamil Nadu; tradition gives him 120 years (1017–1137), while scholars place him c. 1077–1157. Trained first in Advaita under Yādavaprakāśa, he broke with his teacher over the meaning of the Upanishads and became leader of the Śrīvaiṣṇava community at the temple of Srirangam, where the devotional poems of the Tamil Āḻvār saints were central to worship. His Śrībhāṣya on the Brahma Sūtra is the classic statement of Viśiṣṭādvaita, “qualified non-dualism”.

Rāmānuja agreed with Śaṅkara that Brahman is the one reality, but not that it is featureless or that the world is illusion. Brahman is a personal God, Viṣṇu–Nārāyaṇa, with infinite good qualities. Souls and matter are real, and they relate to God as a body relates to its soul: distinct from him, dependent on him and wholly animated by him. Liberation is not dissolving into undifferentiated consciousness but eternal, loving service of God, reached through devotion (bhakti) and, above all, surrender (prapatti) to his grace.
