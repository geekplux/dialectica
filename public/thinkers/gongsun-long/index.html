Dialectica / Thinkers / Gongsun Long

China & East Asia · c. 320–250 BCE

Gongsun Long

Disputer famous for “a white horse is not a horse”

Argued that a term for a compound (“white horse”) is not the same as a term for a kind (“horse”), probing how names pick out attributes and things.

Life and thought

Gongsun Long, from the state of Zhao, lived roughly 320–250 BCE and was a retainer of Lord Pingyuan, a powerful Zhao prince. Early sources record him urging kings to lay down their arms, an echo of Mohist ideas. Of the book under his name, a handful of chapters survive, and scholars debate how many are genuinely his.

He is famous for the thesis that “a white horse is not a horse”. If you ask for a horse, a yellow or a black one will do; if you ask for a white horse, they will not. So what the term “white horse” picks out differs from what “horse” picks out, and the two terms cannot be identical. In another dialogue he argues that hardness and whiteness in a stone are separate, since sight gives the white but not the hard, and touch the hard but not the white. Xunzi and the Zhuangzi dismissed such arguments as sophistry; twentieth-century scholars read them anew as early Chinese reflection on reference, kinds and logic.

On the record

Uncertain

Hui Shi is shown as an influence on Gongsun Long.

Both are counted among the disputers (bianzhe) and the later tradition groups them as the School of Names, but no source records that they met or that Gongsun Long learned from Hui Shi. The tie marks a shared line of argument, not a documented contact.
