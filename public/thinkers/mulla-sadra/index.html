Dialectica / Thinkers / Mullā Ṣadrā

The Islamic world · c. 1571–1636

Mullā Ṣadrā

Safavid philosopher of Shiraz, founder of Transcendent Theosophy

Existence, not essence, is what is real; it is one reality in many degrees. Things change in their very substance, so the whole world is in constant motion toward perfection.

Life and thought

Ṣadr al-Dīn Shīrāzī, known as Mullā Ṣadrā, was born around 1571 in Shiraz, into a wealthy family, and studied in Isfahan, the Safavid capital, with Mīr Dāmād and Shaykh Bahāʾī. Attacked by jurists for his views, he withdrew for years to the village of Kahak near Qom, where he began his great Four Journeys in 1606. Later he taught at a new college in Shiraz, finishing the book there in 1628. He died in Basra in 1635–36 while travelling on pilgrimage, though a later tradition gives 1640.

Earlier philosophers argued whether essence or existence is primary. Ṣadrā held that existence is what is real and essences are only the outlines our minds draw around it. Existence is one reality in degrees of intensity, like light in Suhrawardī, from God down to matter. From this he drew a startling thesis: things change not only in their qualities but in their very substance, so the whole world is in constant motion toward perfection. His synthesis still shapes philosophy teaching in Iran.

On the record

Corrected

Mullā Ṣadrā died in 1640.

1640 (1050 AH) is the traditional date, but there is no clear evidence for it. His grandson reported that he died in Basra in 1045 AH (1635–36), on the way to or from the pilgrimage, and two notes in his manuscripts support this, as does the 1635 completion date of his last works. The Asfār, begun in 1606, was completed in Shiraz in 1628.

Sources: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Mulla Sadra”
