Dialectica / Thinkers / Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel

Early modern Europe · 1770–1831

Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel

German philosopher of spirit, history and dialectic

Reality is spirit coming to know itself through a dialectical process in which every limited position produces its own contradiction and is overcome. World history is the progress of the consciousness of freedom.

Life and thought

Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel studied at the Tübingen seminary with Schelling and Hölderlin and worked for years as a private tutor. He finished the Phenomenology of Spirit at Jena in 1806 as Napoleon’s army fought outside the town. He then edited a newspaper in Bamberg and ran a school in Nuremberg, before professorships at Heidelberg and, from 1818, Berlin, where he became the most influential philosopher in Germany. He died in 1831, probably of cholera.

Hegel’s guiding claim is that “the true is the whole”. Any limited idea or way of life, followed to its consequences, runs into contradictions that force it to be taken up into a richer one; he called this movement dialectic. The Phenomenology follows consciousness through such stages, including the struggle between master and slave, until spirit comes to understand itself. History too is the story of freedom becoming conscious: in the ancient East one was free, in Greece and Rome some, in the modern world all. His followers split into conservatives and radicals, and Marx, Kierkegaard and the pragmatists all began by arguing with him.

On the record

Confirmed

Schopenhauer scheduled his Berlin lectures at the same hour as Hegel’s.

In March 1820 Schopenhauer, newly licensed to teach in Berlin, scheduled his class at the same time as Hegel’s popular lectures; few students came. He had studied in Berlin in 1811–13, where his lecturers included Fichte.

Sources: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Arthur Schopenhauer”
