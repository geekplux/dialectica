Dialectica / Thinkers / Gilbert Ryle

Analytic & pragmatist · 1900–1976

Gilbert Ryle

Oxford philosopher who mocked the “ghost in the machine”

Treating the mind as a hidden inner thing is a “category mistake”, like looking for the university over and above its colleges; talk of minds is talk of abilities and dispositions to behave.

Life and thought

Gilbert Ryle (1900–1976) spent his whole career at Oxford, as a tutor at Christ Church and from 1945 as Waynflete Professor of Metaphysical Philosophy. He edited the journal Mind from 1947 to 1971, helped create the BPhil degree that drew graduate students from around the world, and more than anyone made postwar Oxford a centre of philosophy; Ayer and, later, Dennett were among his students.

The Concept of Mind (1949) attacks what Ryle called “the dogma of the Ghost in the Machine”: Descartes’ picture of a private inner mind steering a body. The picture rests on a category mistake, like that of a visitor shown Oxford’s colleges, libraries and laboratories who then asks, “But where is the University?” To say that someone is intelligent, vain or speaks French is not to report hidden inner events but to describe how they are disposed to act. Knowing how to do something is not a matter of knowing that certain facts are true. Critics called this behaviourism; Ryle said he was mapping the logic of our mental concepts.

On the record

Uncertain

The Chinese title of Ryle’s Dilemmas (1954) is given as 《两难》.

Dilemmas collects Ryle’s Tarner Lectures on apparent conflicts between theories. No published Chinese translation could be found, so 《两难》 remains a descriptive rendering of the title.

Sources: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Gilbert Ryle”

Confirmed

Ryle was Ayer’s tutor at Christ Church and suggested he go to Vienna in 1933.

Ryle was one of Ayer’s tutors at Christ Church, Oxford; having met Schlick in 1930, he encouraged Ayer to attend the Vienna Circle, which Ayer did from January to March 1933.

Sources: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Alfred Jules Ayer”
