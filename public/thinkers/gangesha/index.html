India · fl. 14th c., Mithilā

Gaṅgeśa Upādhyāya

Founder of Navya-Nyāya, the ‘new logic’

He reorganised Nyāya around the four means of knowledge and developed a precise technical language of relations and absences, used to define invariable concomitance (vyāpti) with new rigour.

Life and thought

Gaṅgeśa Upādhyāya lived in the first half of the 14th century in Mithilā, in today’s Bihar, which for the next centuries was the great centre of Nyāya learning, later rivalled by Navadvīpa in Bengal. Almost nothing is known of his life. His one great book, the Jewel of Reflection on Reality (Tattvacintāmaṇi), launched Navya-Nyāya, the “new logic”, and became the most commented-on text of later Indian philosophy.

Instead of following the Nyāya Sūtra topic by topic, Gaṅgeśa reorganised the whole subject around the four means of knowledge and treated each with new precision. His key tool was a technical language for describing cognitions in terms of relations, qualifiers and absences—something like a formal logic built out of Sanskrit. With it he tested and rejected one proposed definition after another of invariable concomitance (vyāpti), the relation that makes inference work, before giving his own. This style of exact analysis spread from Nyāya into law, grammar and poetics, and dominated Sanskrit scholarship until the colonial era.
