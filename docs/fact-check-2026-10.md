## Still open

- `tie:empedocles>gorgias`: The report goes back to the Hellenistic biographer Satyrus, quoted by Diogenes Laertius (8.58), and is repeated by later writers. The two were near contemporaries, and modern reference works range from accepting it to calling it a biographer’s tale; the atlas keeps the tie but marks it as a report.
- `thinker:leucippus`: Nothing firm is known of his dates or birthplace; ancient reports make him a pupil of Zeno of Elea and the teacher of Democritus. Epicurus apparently denied there had been a philosopher Leucippus, and some 19th-century scholars agreed, but Aristotle and Theophrastus treat him as real. The one surviving sentence, from a work On Mind, says nothing happens in vain (or at random) but everything from reason and by necessity.
- `thinker:al-ashari`: Neither book can be dated. The Maqālāt was probably begun in his Muʿtazili years, on the model of al-Balkhī’s book of the same title, and revised later, though al-Dhahabī placed it in his last years. Scholars disagree on whether al-Ibāna came right after his break with the Muʿtazila (c. 912–13) or near the end of his life, and its printed text is suspected of later interpolation. The atlas now gives no year for either.
- `tie:merleau-ponty>fanon`: The dates fit: Fanon studied medicine in Lyon after the war, and Merleau-Ponty taught psychology at the University of Lyon from 1945 to 1949. That Fanon sat in on his lectures is repeated in biographies and reference works, but the encyclopedia entries we checked do not confirm it; what is certain is that Black Skin, White Masks draws on Merleau-Ponty’s phenomenology of the body.
- `thinker:ayer`: The book is Ayer’s 1956 study of scepticism, perception, memory and other minds. No published Chinese translation could be found, so 《知识问题》 remains a descriptive rendering of the title, not the name of an edition.
- `thinker:ryle`: Dilemmas collects Ryle’s Tarner Lectures on apparent conflicts between theories. No published Chinese translation could be found, so 《两难》 remains a descriptive rendering of the title.
- `tie:hui-shi>gongsun-long`: Both are counted among the disputers (bianzhe) and the later tradition groups them as the School of Names, but no source records that they met or that Gongsun Long learned from Hui Shi. The tie marks a shared line of argument, not a documented contact.

### Proposals not applied

Rejected because the audit misread the data: Chinese work titles “missing a year” (the year is one shared field),
Zhuangzi’s Inner Chapters (290 BCE is before, not after, his death), Duns Scotus’ 《〈箴言集〉评注》 (箴言集 is the
usual Chinese for Lombard’s Sentences), Śaṅkara’s “older scholarship”, and Rousseau leaving Geneva “at sixteen” (he
was fifteen in March 1728). Left as they are because both versions are defensible: work years for Gorgias,
Anaxagoras, the Ācārāṅga, Plotinus’ Enneads, Hegel’s Philosophy of Right, Boethius’ Consolation, al-Ghazālī’s
Deliverance, Buridan’s Summulae and Aristotle’s Ethics; plotted birth and death years for Jaimini, Vasubandhu,
Umāsvāti, Patañjali, Kumārila and Gaṅgeśa; the ends of Cārvāka and the start of German idealism; Han Fei’s mission
in 233 or 234 BCE; Ayer’s chair in 1958 or 1959; Lévi-Strauss in São Paulo; and the notes on Nietzsche’s sister,
Yājñavalkya, al-Kindī and Fanon. Ties with no note (Locke and Berkeley to Hume, Locke to Rousseau and Montesquieu)
await notes. Thirty units (ties 12–35 and the seven questions) were not checked before the run stopped; see the
next section.
